# The game of hats: where we stand (2026-11-28)

A map of the results, what each one rests on, and what was compared with
the literature. Papers read in the source: Buhler, Freiling, Graham,
Kariv, Roche, Tiefenbruck, Van Alten, Yeroshkin, arXiv:1407.4711v8
([BFGKRTVY]); Bouquet et al., arXiv:2508.01737v2 ([Bo25]; the sections
on recursive strategies, on finite stacks and on `p != 1/2` read on
2026-11-28). Not read: Heilman and Tamuz; the note
github.com/yangsong97/levine-hat-bounds beyond its README.

"Lean" means: stated and proved in `formal/hats`, in the audit, with the
standard axioms only. "Counted" means: a count by two programs written
separately. "Program" means: one program, or samples.

A remark that the reading of [Bo25] adds. Their Lemma: for every `h`,
`|7/20 - V_{2,h}| >= 1 / (5 * 4^h)` (the best score is a whole number of
pairs). Their last theorem on finite stacks:
`V_{2,h} >= 7/20 - C_eps / (4^(1 - eps))^h`. The Carter strategies give
more, for every `h`: `V_{2,h} >= 7/20 - 3 / (5 * 4^h)` (even `h`) and
`7/20 - 2 / (5 * 4^h)` (odd `h`), in Lean. Together: if the conjecture
`V = 7/20` holds, the gap is between 1 and 3 times `1 / (5 * 4^h)`; and
the Carter scores are the largest whole numbers below `7 * 4^h / 20`, so
any better strategy on `h` hats would pass `7/20` (our Proposition 12).

## 3b. Papers read on 2026-11-28 (sources from arXiv and GitHub)

- Heilman and Tamuz, arXiv:2503.09042: two players, fair coin; an upper
  bound by Fourier analysis (Chang's lemma), 0.374 in the first version
  (0.37193 as quoted by Yang Song), with no computer. No lower bound, no
  other `p`, no more players.
- Alon, Friedgut, Kalai, Kindler, arXiv:2103.01541 and 2208.06858: the
  best win rate decreases strictly with the number of players; links
  with independent sets in graphs and with sets that meet every largest
  independent set. No explicit bound for three or four players.
- Yang Song, github.com/yangsong97/levine-hat-bounds (paper.md):
  `V <= 17/48` through the same relaxations `H_d`; `max H_4 = 57/160`
  with 11 classes, as we found; an earlier write-up of the same author
  had 3795217/10582656 = 0.358626. Two players only. The note does not
  relate `H_d` to the Carter numbers; its open question 3 is whether
  `max H_d` tends to `V`. Our inequality `H_d >= ` (Carter number) and
  the four hint-only classes bear on it.

None of them has the substitution theorem, the bounds for three and four
players, the white resets for `p != 1/2`, or the reading of the hint game
by lines.

## 3c. Documents received from the author, read the same day

See `literature.md`. Two of our claims had to be narrowed: the Carter
scores on every number of hats and their link with the conjecture are in
the book of Velleman and Wagon (2020, statement 76.2, a sketch of proof;
Yeroshkin's remark); the reset on white only is in Wagon's notes of 2014
for fair coins. The article and the paper now say so. One claim of
those notes, 64/225 for three players, is above our bound and cannot be
right with the reset of Buhler et al.; it is discussed in the paper.

## 4. What is not secured

- The literature search is by web queries and five papers read (Section
  3b). Before any public claim: the papers that cite [BFGKRTVY], which
  were not listed systematically.
- The counts of the tables on seven and eight hats (three players) are
  by programs; those on six hats (three players) and on three and four
  hats (four players) are done by Lean's kernel.
- Six hats, two players: 1,433 is not proved best.
- The formula of the hint bounds for `d >= 5`, and whether hint-only
  solutions can do better than the game there.
