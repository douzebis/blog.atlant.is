# The game of hats: where we stand (2026-11-28)

A map of the results, what each one rests on, and what was compared with
the literature. Papers read in the source: Buhler, Freiling, Graham,
Kariv, Roche, Tiefenbruck, Van Alten, Yeroshkin, arXiv:1407.4711v8
([BFGKRTVY]); Bouquet et al., arXiv:2508.01737v2 ([Bo25]; the sections
on recursive strategies, on finite stacks and on `p != 1/2` read on
2026-11-28). Not read: Heilman and Tamuz; the note
github.com/yangsong97/levine-hat-bounds beyond its README.

"Lean" means: stated and proved in `formal/hats`, in the audit, with the
standard axioms only. "Counted" means: a count by two programs written
separately. "Program" means: one program, or samples.

A remark that the reading of [Bo25] adds. Their Lemma: for every `h`,
`|7/20 - V_{2,h}| >= 1 / (5 * 4^h)` (the best score is a whole number of
pairs). Their last theorem on finite stacks:
`V_{2,h} >= 7/20 - C_eps / (4^(1 - eps))^h`. The Carter strategies give
more, for every `h`: `V_{2,h} >= 7/20 - 3 / (5 * 4^h)` (even `h`) and
`7/20 - 2 / (5 * 4^h)` (odd `h`), in Lean. Together: if the conjecture
`V = 7/20` holds, the gap is between 1 and 3 times `1 / (5 * 4^h)`; and
the Carter scores are the largest whole numbers below `7 * 4^h / 20`, so
any better strategy on `h` hats would pass `7/20` (our Proposition 12).

## 4. What is not secured

- The literature search is by web queries and two papers read. Before any
  public claim: Heilman and Tamuz, the papers that cite [BFGKRTVY], and
  the note of Yang Song in full.
- The counts of the tables (three and four players) are by programs. A
  check inside Lean is possible for small tables only.
- Six hats, two players: 1,433 is not proved best.
- The formula of the hint bounds for `d >= 5`, and whether hint-only
  solutions can do better than the game there.
